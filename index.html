Customer Experience

The bill nobody could read

Billing was our highest call volume — roughly 9,750 calls a month. I audited ~10,000 transcripts and found repeating confusion patterns no FAQ would fix. I evaluated multiple vendors and chose AWS text-to-speech over heavier builds — the transcripts said customers needed clarity, not conversation — then iterated the explainer against measured behavior and qual feedback.

15% of billing support calls deflected.

The “flaky network” that wasn’t

Recurring VoIP drops across ~60K calls a month. Root-cause analysis across SIP signaling, terminal logs, and customer complaints pointed at bandwidth-request behavior during peak congestion — not capacity. I drove firmware validation and a staged rollout with Engineering.

Dropped calls down ~10%, CSAT up ~15%.

Churn you can engineer down

Directed the cross-functional rollout of traffic prioritization, congestion throttling, and satellite load balancing — network capabilities sold internally as experience improvements, measured like it.

Churn down 10%, CSAT from 80 to 95.

The half-million hiding in the records

Ran SLA dashboards, process audits, and invoice reviews across 10+ vendor partners. Found ~4,000 site-record mismatches adding up to roughly $40K a month in operational inefficiency — then drove the corrective actions and contract changes to kill it.

~$500K in annualized inefficiency recovered.

A review cadence vendors respect

Weekly and monthly business reviews run on industry-standard frameworks — KPI and SLA scorecards, not vibes. Vendors know exactly where they stand, and so do we.

Governance rhythm across 10+ partners.

Buying the Bill Explainer right

Evaluated multiple vendors for the Interactive Bill Explainer, negotiated on cost, right-sized for actual usage, and cut operational waste out of the contract.

Lower cost, right-sized from day one.

What I’d do differently: start the invoice audits in month one, not quarter three. The mismatches were compounding the whole time we weren’t looking.
